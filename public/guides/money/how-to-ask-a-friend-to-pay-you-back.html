How to Ask a Friend to Pay You Back (Without Making It Weird)

To ask a friend to pay you back, send a short, friendly text with the exact amount, what it was for, and your payment link: Just the $40 from drinks at McKinley's, here's my Venmo. No reply in a week? Send one follow-up, then decide whether to let it go.

  1. Ask by text, not in person.
  2. Give the exact amount and what it was for.
  3. Include your payment link in the same message.
  4. One follow-up after a week; not three.
  5. In future, lend small amounts only if you'd be fine never seeing them again.

Updated October 9, 2026 · By the DeftBrain team

Asking a friend to repay a small loan works best as a short, specific, low-pressure message sent soon after the expense: the exact amount, what it covered, and a payment handle so they can pay in one step. A text gives both of you room to respond without an awkward pause. If you use a payment app, the FTC suggests double-checking the recipient's details before money moves, because app payments can be hard to reverse.

You covered drinks six weeks ago. They said they'd Venmo you. They didn't. Each time you've seen them since, you've debated bringing it up and haven't, because saying 'you owe me $40' feels aggressive over a number that isn't life-changing. The longer it waits, the heavier it feels, which is why a plain message tonight is easier than the perfect one later.

How to do it
1

How do you ask a friend for money they owe you?

Asking in person can feel heavier than it is, and the pause after the ask is what makes it awkward. A text is often easier for both of you. Something like: 'Hey, not sure if it slipped your mind, but you owe me about $40 from drinks last month. No rush, just flagging.' The tone is casual, the amount is specific, and there's no deadline pressure.

2

What should you say when asking for money back?

Vague messages fail. Hey did you ever pay me back for that thing makes the friend defensive and unsure how to respond. The $40 from drinks at McKinley's is concrete and impossible to misinterpret. Specifics also subtly remind the friend that you are not making this up — there is a real evening, a real bill, a real number. Specificity makes the request easy to answer.

3

How do you make it easy for a friend to pay you back?

Include your payment handle or link in the same message. A common reason small debts go unpaid is friction: they have to remember, find your handle, type the amount and add a note. Bring it down to one tap: 'Send to @yourname when you get a sec.' Repayments that would happen at all tend to happen soon after an easy prompt; the longer it sits, the easier it is to forget.

4

How many times should you remind a friend about money they owe?

If they don't respond within a week, one short follow-up is reasonable: 'Hey, just bumping this up.' After that, many people stop. Repeated reminders can shift things from friend reminding friend to creditor chasing debtor. At that point you're deciding between the money and the friendship, and for a small amount, the friendship often matters more. For a larger amount, a direct conversation may be worth having.

5

How do you avoid lending money to friends you won't get back?

One approach is to set a personal threshold, say $50, below which you treat any loan to a friend as something you'd be fine never seeing again. Above it, settle up at the table before anyone leaves. Small loans become gifts in your head, large ones become clear transactions, and the uncomfortable middle mostly disappears.
