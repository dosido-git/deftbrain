How to Dispute a Bill You Do Not Recognize

Five steps from "what is this" to "removed from my account" — without missing the deadline that locks you in.

Updated October 5, 2026 · By the DeftBrain team

An envelope shows up. The logo is unfamiliar. The amount is odd. You open it and there is a bill from a company you have never heard of, or one you used once two years ago, for charges that do not match anything you bought. Your first thought: scam. Your second thought: what if it is real and I ignore it and it goes to collections.

Both possibilities exist. The good news is that the same first step handles both: dispute it in writing, on time, with the right language. After that you find out which it was.

The dispute sequence, in the order that protects you.

How to do it
1

Find the dispute deadline before you do anything else

The deadline depends on where the charge showed up. If it is on your credit card statement, the federal Fair Credit Billing Act gives you 60 days from the statement containing the error to dispute it in writing with the card issuer. If it is a direct bill or invoice from a company, there is no federal 60-day clock — the window is whatever the company's own policy and your state's consumer law say, so look at the bill for a "billing rights" or "dispute" section, check the company policy online, and dispute promptly either way. Waiting is what turns "I do not owe this" into "I do, actually, because I waited."

2

Send the dispute in writing, not by phone

A phone call to a customer service rep does not legally count as a dispute. You need a letter, an email, or a portal submission with a record. Use the address listed for billing inquiries — not the payment address, which goes to a different department. Keep it short: "I am disputing the charge of [amount] on the statement dated [date]. I have no record of authorizing or receiving this service. Please provide documentation showing the basis for this charge."

3

Do not pay the disputed amount while it is being investigated

You can pay the rest of the bill (the parts you do recognize) — just not the disputed line. If the charge is on your credit card, the Fair Credit Billing Act does the heavy lifting: the issuer cannot collect the disputed amount or report it as delinquent while the dispute is open, must acknowledge your dispute within 30 days, and must resolve it within two billing cycles (no more than 90 days). For a direct bill, there is no equivalent federal clock — but if the account gets handed to a debt collector, the FDCPA kicks in: you have 30 days from the collector's first written notice to demand validation of the debt in writing, and the collector must pause collection until it provides it. Many states add their own protections on top; check your state attorney general's consumer pages.

4

If they cannot prove the charge, push for it to come off

When you dispute and ask for documentation, you are asking the company to show what you actually agreed to or received. On a credit card dispute, the issuer must investigate and remove billing errors it cannot substantiate. With a direct biller, there is no federal rule forcing removal — but a company that cannot produce a signed agreement, service log, or delivery confirmation has no case to enforce, and a collector that cannot validate the debt has to stop collecting. This is where most fraudulent or mistaken charges fold. The company assumed you would pay without asking, and asking is enough. If the charge is a medical one, reading the itemized hospital bill line by line is usually the fastest way to find what is wrong.

5

Escalate if they ignore the dispute

If the company stalls, ignores you, or threatens collections while a dispute is open, file a complaint with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, your state attorney general, or the BBB. For credit card charges, dispute through the card issuer instead — issuers are required to credit your account during the investigation. Most issuers reverse fraudulent charges quickly because they have stronger leverage with merchants than you do alone.
