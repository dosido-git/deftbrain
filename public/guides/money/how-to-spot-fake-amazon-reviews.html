How to Spot Fake Amazon Reviews

To spot fake Amazon reviews, look at the shape of the ratings rather than the average, sort by date to find bursts, read the one- and two-star reviews first, search for repeated phrases, and check whether a few reviewers have histories that look like real people.

  1. Look at the rating distribution, not the average.
  2. Sort by date and look for bursts.
  3. Read the one- and two-star reviews first.
  4. Search for phrases repeated across reviews.
  5. Check a few reviewers' other reviews.

Updated October 9, 2026 · By the DeftBrain team

Fake or incentivized reviews tend to leave patterns: unusual rating distributions, bursts of reviews in a short window, repeated phrasing and reviewer accounts with odd histories. The FTC advises watching for bursts of reviews, checking whether a reviewer has written other reviews, and not relying on star ratings alone, while noting that individual fake reviews can be nearly impossible to identify for sure. Since October 2024, an FTC rule has banned fake reviews and paying for reviews that express a particular sentiment.

A product has 4,200 five-star reviews, and the number feels like proof. Then you read a few: the same praise, the same length, the same eager phrasing. Some are probably fake, and you can't tell which. You don't have to identify each one; the patterns tell you how much to trust the pool.

How to do it
1

How can you tell if Amazon reviews are fake?

Look at the bar chart of ratings, not the average. Many genuine products show lots of five-stars, some fours, fewer threes, and a real tail of one- and two-stars. If a product's ratings are almost all fives and ones with very little in between, that pattern is worth noticing: the five-stars may be doing too much work. It isn't proof, but it's a reason to read more carefully.

2

Does the timing of reviews show if they're fake?

It can. Sort reviews by date. Genuine products tend to gather reviews at a fairly steady pace. Products with paid review campaigns sometimes show clusters, such as hundreds of reviews in one week, then little, then another cluster. The FTC lists a burst of reviews over a short period as a possible warning sign. The pattern over time can tell you what the average won't.

3

Are negative reviews more trustworthy than positive ones?

Often, but not always. Fake reviews are usually positive, though competitors sometimes post fake negative ones, which the FTC's rule also bans. Read one- and two-star reviews looking for specific, repeated problems: the zipper broke, the cable shorted, the size ran small. If several reviewers describe the same concrete failure, take it seriously regardless of the average.

4

Do fake reviews use the same words?

Pick a distinctive phrase from a five-star review — "truly exceeded my expectations" or "this is a game-changer" — and search for it across the review section. If the same phrase appears in many reviews on the same product, you've caught a fake-review template. Real customers rarely use the exact same phrasing independently. Other tells in this category: the same name dropped repeatedly, the same competitor product mentioned by name, the same usage scenario described in identical detail.

5

How do you check if an Amazon reviewer is real?

Click on a five-star reviewer and look at their other reviews. Real reviewers have a mix: some products they loved, some they didn't, normal categories, normal lifestyle. Fake reviewers have patterns that give them away — only ever five-star reviews, dozens of reviews in unrelated categories within the same week, the same writing style on radically different products. Two minutes spent on the reviewer's profile is more diagnostic than ten minutes on the product page.
