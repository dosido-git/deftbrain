How to Say No to a Timeshare Presentation (And Actually Get Out)

To say no to a timeshare presentation, decide before you arrive that you will not buy, state your time limit at the start, answer every pitch with one sentence, and stand up when time is up to collect the promised gift. If you sign, check your cancellation period at once.

  1. Decide in advance: no purchase today.
  2. Say your hard stop out loud at the start.
  3. Repeat one sentence; don't give reasons.
  4. Expect a new, friendlier closer partway through.
  5. Stand up at your time limit and ask for the gift.

Updated October 9, 2026 · By the DeftBrain team

Timeshare presentations are sales meetings, often offered in exchange for a gift or discount, that commonly run longer than advertised. The FTC warns that promoters may keep people waiting, rotate several salespeople, and say an offer is only good today, hoping buyers sign to get out. The FTC advises not acting quickly or under pressure on a commitment that can last decades. Many states give timeshare buyers a short right to cancel after signing; the length varies by state and should be stated in the contract.

You took the free dinner and the promise of a 'short ninety-minute presentation.' Three hours later, you've met four salespeople, each nicer than the last, and the price keeps dropping while the features grow. You're tired, and the pen is closer than it was. Saying no is possible; it helps to know the structure in advance.

How to do it
1

How do you go to a timeshare presentation without buying?

The single most important move happens before the presentation starts. Make a firm decision, with whoever you're attending with, that you are not buying anything today, full stop, regardless of how appealing the offer becomes. The presentation is designed to make the offer appealing — that's its only job. Reconsidering inside the room is the entire game; refusing to reconsider, no matter what number they get to, is how you walk out with the gift card and nothing else.

2

How long do timeshare presentations really last?

Presentations advertised as 90 minutes often run longer. Set your limit in the room before the pitch starts: 'We have plans at 1 PM, so we need to be out by 12:30.' Have a real reason, like a reservation or a flight, and say it early. They may still try to extend, but now the question of extending is out in the open rather than implied, which makes it easier to hold.

3

What should you say to turn down a timeshare offer?

Presentations are built around drawing out your concerns so each can be answered: too expensive (a lower price), don't travel enough (a flexible program), need to think (the deal expires today). You don't have to engage. Pick one sentence, 'We're not buying anything today,' and use it for every push. The first time it feels rude; by the fifth it feels routine.

4

Who is the closer at a timeshare presentation?

Partway through, your salesperson may leave to 'check with someone,' and a new person arrives, often more relaxed and personable. This is commonly the closer, whose role is the warmer, more personal part of the pitch: asking about your family, sharing something personal, saying they understand your hesitation. It's the same pitch in a different voice. You can be friendly back without lowering your guard.

5

How do you leave a timeshare presentation that won't end?

When your time limit passes, stand up and say: 'We need to head out. Please point us to where we collect the promised gift.' If they say 'just one more thing,' answer: 'No, we need to leave now.' If they keep going, walk out. It's uncomfortable, but it's brief. If you did sign something under pressure, read the contract for the cancellation period right away; many states set one, and it's often only a few days, usually requiring written notice.
