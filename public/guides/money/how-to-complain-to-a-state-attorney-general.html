How to Complain to a State Attorney General

To complain to a state attorney general, use the consumer complaint form on your state AG website (USA.gov and NAAG link to every state). Give a short timeline, name the law if you know it, attach documents, and state the outcome you want. The office may mediate.

  1. File with your own state's AG; the company's state may also accept it.
  2. Open with a five-sentence timeline.
  3. Name the consumer law that seems to apply, if you know it.
  4. Attach receipts, emails, screenshots and call notes.
  5. Ask for a specific outcome, such as a refund of a set amount.

Updated October 9, 2026 · By the DeftBrain team

State attorneys general run consumer protection offices that accept complaints from residents, usually through an online form. Many forward complaints to the business or offer informal mediation, and they use patterns of complaints to decide what to investigate. They generally cannot act as your private lawyer. USA.gov and the National Association of Attorneys General both link to every state's consumer office. Procedures differ by state, so read your AG's instructions before filing.

You've spent weeks on this. The company won't respond, the amount is real money, and you've done the calls, the emails and the review sites. A state attorney general's office has something those don't: enforcement authority. A clear, well-documented complaint gives that office the most to work with.

How to do it
1

Which state attorney general should you file a complaint with?

Generally, file with the attorney general of the state where you live. Some AG offices also accept complaints from out-of-state consumers about businesses based in their state, so the company's home state can be a second option. Most offices have an online consumer complaint form; some also accept mail. Use the form they provide rather than a generic letter. USA.gov's state consumer office lookup and NAAG's directory will get you to the right page.

2

How do you write a complaint to the attorney general?

Consumer protection staff read many complaints. Open with one paragraph that captures the whole situation in about five sentences: what you bought, what happened, what you tried, how the company responded, and what you're asking for. Someone should be able to understand your complaint from that paragraph alone. Tight summaries are easier to act on than long, emotional ones.

3

Should you mention specific laws in an attorney general complaint?

AG complaints land harder when they reference the law the company appears to have violated. Common ones: state Unfair and Deceptive Acts and Practices statutes, the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act for product warranty issues, the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, the Telephone Consumer Protection Act. You do not have to be a lawyer — just naming the relevant law signals that you have done your homework and tells the AG which division to route the complaint to.

4

What documents should you include with an attorney general complaint?

AGs cannot act on undocumented claims. Attach: receipts, contracts, terms of service screenshots, every email and chat transcript, photos of any physical issue, call notes with dates and rep names, and any prior complaint filings (BBB, CFPB). Even if some of these feel redundant, a complete file moves faster through the AG’s pipeline because the staff does not have to come back asking for documents.

5

What happens after you file a complaint with the attorney general?

Be specific about what you want: 'I am requesting a refund of $X' gives the office something concrete to pass on. Many AG offices send your complaint to the business and ask for a response; some offer mediation. Outcomes vary. Individual complaints aren't always resolved, but they become part of the record the office uses to spot patterns and decide on investigations. Keep copies of everything, and note any case number you're given.
