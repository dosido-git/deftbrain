How to Get Things Done When You Have No Energy

When you have no energy, skip the hardest task, pick one small thing that fits the energy you have, cut its scope, and stop after one win. Water or a few minutes outside can buy a little clarity. If tiredness lasts weeks despite rest, see a doctor.

  1. Don't start with the hardest task.
  2. Pick one option and don't reconsider it.
  3. Use a quick reset to buy a few clear minutes.
  4. Shrink one task to its smallest useful version.
  5. Stop after one win and rest.

Updated October 9, 2026 · By the DeftBrain team

On a low-energy day, matching the task to the energy you have works better than pushing harder. Practical steps are choosing small, low-demand tasks, cutting their scope, using a brief reset such as water, fresh air or a short walk, and stopping after one finished task. Ongoing tiredness has many possible causes, including poor sleep, stress, depression and conditions such as anemia or thyroid problems; the NHS advises seeing a doctor if tiredness isn't relieved by rest.

It's 2 p.m., you've stared at the same document for forty minutes, and you can't make yourself care about anything on the list. Pushing harder doesn't work when the tank is empty, and neither does waiting for motivation. Choosing the right small action does.

How to do it
1

What should you do first when you have no energy?

When you have no energy, the worst move is staring down the most demanding item on the list; even thinking about it uses up what little you have. Be honest about your energy right now, notice where you are and what's within reach, and look for tasks that fit that state: low-stakes, familiar, short. The right list for today will probably not include the hard thing.

2

How do you stop overthinking which task to do?

Deciding takes energy you don't have. Pick the first reasonable small task and do that one. Don't modify it, renegotiate it or check whether something else would be better. Choosing quickly and moving matters more on a low day than choosing perfectly.

3

What can you do for a quick energy boost?

If even the top pick feels like too much, use a quick hit instead. Cold water on the face. Five minutes outside. A glass of water. These do not restore energy, but they buy you a window of slightly improved clarity. That window is what you spend on one small task, not the giant one.

4

How do you get things done when you're exhausted?

Instead of write the report, write one paragraph. Instead of clean the kitchen, do the dishes in the sink. Instead of reply to all the emails, reply to one. Low-energy productivity is built on radically reducing scope. The win is finishing something, not finishing the right thing.

5

When should you stop working on a low-energy day?

Once you've finished one small task, it's tempting to ride the momentum into a second and third. Often it's wiser not to. On a no-energy day, one win is the goal. Rest afterward so tomorrow can be a normal day. If low-energy days have become most days for several weeks, that's worth raising with a doctor, since persistent tiredness can have medical causes.
