The Smallest Possible First Step for Writing a Book

The smallest first step for writing a book is to open a blank document, name it with a working title and save it. Then, in short sessions, write one sentence on what it's about, list five things it should contain, write 200 rough words, and schedule the next 200.

  1. Create and name the document.
  2. Write one sentence on what it's about.
  3. List five things it should contain.
  4. Write 200 rough words on the easiest one.
  5. Put the next session on your calendar.

Updated October 9, 2026 · By the DeftBrain team

Starting a book is easier when the first steps are too small to avoid: create a document with a working title, write one sentence describing the book, list five things it should include, then write 200 rough words on the easiest of them. Scheduling a regular writing time keeps it going. Small daily amounts add up: 200 words a day comes to about 73,000 words in a year, the length of many novels.

You've wanted to write a book for years. You've had ideas, bought books about writing books, and not written a sentence of your own. Every time you think about starting, you think about the whole book, which is exactly why nothing happens.

How to do it
1

What is the very first step to writing a book?

Don't write anything yet. Open a blank document — Word, Google Docs, whatever. Name it with the working title of the book, even if the title is rough or temporary. Save it. Close the document. That's the entire first step. Ninety seconds. The document now exists. The book has gone from imagined to filed. This is a much smaller change than it sounds and a much bigger one than it looks.

2

How do you describe what your book is about?

Reopen the document. Write one sentence: 'This is a book about X.' Or: 'This book is the story of Y.' Or: 'This book argues that Z.' Whatever fits. Don't worry about whether the sentence is right. The first sentence's job is not accuracy; its job is presence. Save and close. Total time elapsed across both sessions: under three minutes. The book now has a one-sentence existence.

3

How do you outline a book when you're just starting?

Open the document a third time. Write a numbered list — five things the book should contain. Topics, chapters, scenes, arguments, characters, whatever fits. The list doesn't have to be the table of contents; it doesn't even have to be in order. It's just five concrete things you can imagine the book including. This is now your skeleton. Most books take this rough form long before they take the actual one.

4

How much should you write in your first writing session?

Pick the easiest item on your list, not the most important or the most exciting. Write 200 words about it, badly. Don't edit, don't research, and don't worry about whether this is the right opening or tone. The point is producing words, not good words. A draft worth keeping on the first try is rare; a draft worth fixing is the whole job, and you now have the start of one.

5

How do you keep writing a book after you start?

Before you close the document, decide when the next 200 words will happen: tomorrow at 7 a.m., Saturday morning, Wednesday lunch. Put it on the calendar. Many writers credit a regular writing time, more than the amount of time, with getting books finished. Small amounts add up: 200 words a day is about 73,000 words in a year.
