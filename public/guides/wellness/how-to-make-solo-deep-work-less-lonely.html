How to make solo deep work less lonely

To make solo deep work less lonely, have one real conversation each day, try silent video coworking, get out of the house twice a day, and find a peer doing similar work to swap progress with. Some loneliness is a normal cost of working alone, not a failure.

  1. Schedule one real conversation every day.
  2. Try silent video coworking.
  3. Get out of the house at least twice a day.
  4. Find one peer doing similar work.
  5. Treat some loneliness as a cost, not a failure.

Updated October 9, 2026 · By the DeftBrain team

Working alone for long stretches can leave people lonely even when the work is going well. The US Surgeon General's 2023 advisory on loneliness and isolation linked a lack of social connection to higher risks for mental health problems and poorer physical health. Practical counterweights for solo workers include a daily conversation, video coworking sessions, regular time out of the house, and a peer doing similar work to share progress with.

You wanted a clear calendar and long, unbroken stretches of thinking. You got it, and months in, the work is fine but the days are too quiet, and finished things land flat because there's no one to tell.

How to do it
1

How do you stop feeling lonely working from home?

If your day has no conversations, you may feel hollow by evening no matter how much got done. Schedule one: coffee with a friend, a video call with a colleague, a walk with a neighbor. Even fifteen minutes breaks the silence. Treat it as part of the working day rather than a luxury. Email and chat help less than hearing a voice and seeing a face. A day with one good conversation often feels very different from a day with none.

2

What is silent video coworking?

There are now coworking video sessions where strangers join, mute their mics, and work together silently for an hour. It sounds odd until you try it. The presence of other faces — even faces you do not know — provides the calibration that solo work lacks. You start when they start. You keep working when they keep working. You take a break when they do. You do not have to talk. The presence does the work.

3

Why does getting out of the house help when you work alone?

Solo workers who rarely leave home often find the isolation wears on them over time. Two outings a day, such as a morning coffee, an afternoon walk or an evening errand, keep you in the world. The work happens at the desk, but the rest of you benefits from daylight, weather, passing faces and the background sense of being among other people.

4

How do you find a peer when you work alone?

Even one person doing similar work, in the same field or at the same stage, can change how solo work feels. Exchange progress notes once a week. Talk about the hard parts. Mark small wins. Former colleagues, online communities for your field, local meetups and alumni groups are common places to find someone. The work stays solo, but it's solo alongside someone who knows what it's like.

5

Is it normal to feel lonely working alone?

Yes. Some loneliness is an honest signal about working alone that you can manage but not eliminate. Treat it like any other working condition: plan around it and build in counterweights. Trying to talk yourself out of feeling it rarely works. People who sustain solo work for years generally haven't stopped feeling lonely; they've built routines that keep it from running the show. If loneliness is persistent and affecting your mood or sleep, it's worth talking to someone about it.

Sources

  • US Department of Health and Human Services: Surgeon General advisory on the epidemic of loneliness and isolation (2023)
