How to Schedule Tasks Based on Your Energy Levels

To schedule by energy, spend a few days noting when you feel sharp and when you feel foggy, put demanding work in your sharpest hours and routine work in your low ones, block your best hours before meetings fill them, and adjust when a week doesn't go as planned.

  1. Notice your daily energy pattern for a few days.
  2. Put hard work in peak hours, admin in dips.
  3. Look at the week's demands before it starts.
  4. Block peak hours before others claim them.
  5. Adjust on off weeks; don't abandon the approach.

Updated October 9, 2026 · By the DeftBrain team

Scheduling by energy means placing demanding work in the hours when you are most alert and routine work in the hours when you are not. Many people notice a mid-afternoon dip, but individual patterns differ, so a few days of noting when you feel sharp or foggy is the best guide. Blocking those peak hours on the calendar before meetings claim them is the practical step that makes the approach work.

You scheduled the hard writing at 4 p.m. and spent forty minutes staring at it after a day of meetings, having done the easy admin in the morning when you were sharp. Most calendars are organized by which slots are free, not by what kind of energy each slot will have.

How to do it
1

How do you find your most productive time of day?

Many people have a fairly predictable daily pattern, often with a peak sometime in the morning and a dip in the early afternoon, though this varies a lot from person to person. Spend three days noticing yours: jot down when you feel sharpest and when you feel foggy. A 30-second note a few times a day is enough to show the pattern within a week.

2

Which tasks should you do when your energy is high?

Deep creative work, hard writing, and complex decisions go in the high-energy windows. Email, slack, admin, and routine meetings go in the low-energy windows. Recovery and breaks go in the troughs. Most people invert this because meetings are scheduled by other people. Defending the high-energy windows is the whole game.

3

How do you plan your week around your energy?

On Sunday or Monday morning, look at what's already on the calendar and mark which days are heavy: back-to-back meetings, travel, late evenings. Those days will have less energy left for demanding work. Place your hardest tasks on the lighter days, in your peak hours, and plan easier work or recovery after the heavy ones. You'll see in advance which days are at risk, before you commit to a deep-work block that has no chance.

4

How do you protect your best hours from meetings?

Once you know your peak windows, hold them. Mark them on the calendar as busy before the meeting requests arrive. Treat them as appointments with the work, not as flexible space. The single biggest leverage point in scheduling by energy is refusing to give your best hours to other people by default.

5

What should you do when your energy doesn't match your plan?

Your energy will not match the forecast some weeks. Travel, sickness, a bad night of sleep all throw it off. When that happens, do not throw out the whole approach. Rebudget against what you actually have, and ship a smaller list. The system survives the bad day if you let it.
