How to Tell When You Are About to Run Out of Energy Before You Do

Early signs that you're running out of energy include a shorter fuse with small things, losing interest in things you usually enjoy, waking tired despite enough sleep, and a week full of social and work commitments you haven't added up. Spotting them early lets you cut back before you crash.

  1. Small irritations start to bother you.
  2. Things you enjoy start to feel like chores.
  3. Sleep stops restoring you.
  4. Your social hours have quietly piled up.
  5. Look at next week's load before it starts.

Updated October 9, 2026 · By the DeftBrain team

Running out of energy usually builds over days rather than arriving suddenly. Common early signs are irritability over small things, reduced enjoyment of usual activities, tiredness that more sleep does not fix, and a schedule packed with commitments you have not counted. Noticing them early leaves room to cancel, shorten or space out plans. Persistent fatigue that rest does not relieve can have medical causes, and the NHS advises seeing a doctor about it.

Fine on Monday, fine on Tuesday, and by Thursday afternoon you can't write a sentence. By Friday you cancel everything. The crash didn't arrive on Thursday; it built all week, and the signs were there days earlier.

How to do it
1

Is being irritable a sign you're running out of energy?

The first sign is not exhaustion — it is a low fuse. The notification that mildly annoys you starts to actually bother you. The text from a friend feels heavy to answer. Your patience for small inconveniences disappears two days before your patience for actual problems does. If you find yourself snapping at small things, you are not just having a bad day; you are running on reserve.

2

Why do things you usually enjoy start to feel like chores?

When the gym you normally like feels like a chore, the friend you enjoy seeing feels like a meeting, and your favorite show feels like work, that's often a sign you need to recharge. The activities haven't changed; your capacity has. Often rest brings the interest back. If it doesn't, and the loss of interest lasts two weeks or more, it's worth talking to a doctor, since it can also be a sign of depression.

3

Why do you wake up tired even after a full night's sleep?

You sleep eight hours and wake tired, then nine and feel the same. Sometimes the deficit isn't sleep but downtime: quiet, low-input time away from people and demands. Sleep restores a lot, but it doesn't replace that. If more sleep isn't fixing your tiredness, try a different kind of recovery. If unrefreshing sleep goes on for weeks, see a doctor, since sleep apnea, anemia, thyroid problems and other conditions can cause it.

4

How many social hours a week is too many?

There's no standard number; it varies a lot between people. What helps is counting. Many people track work hours closely and never add up social time. Three dinners, a weekend brunch, two coffee meetings and a work happy hour can easily add up to many hours of being 'on' on top of a full work week. Count yours for a couple of weeks and notice the total in weeks that ended in a crash. That's a rough personal limit to plan around.

5

How can you plan your week to avoid running out of energy?

Reading your signals after they appear is reactive; looking ahead is preventive. Before the week starts, list its events and mark each as light, moderate or draining. Look for stretches with several draining items in a row and no gap after them. Decide in advance which one you'll skip, shorten or move, and where you'll put an empty evening. You go into the week knowing where the hard stretch is, rather than finding it on Thursday.
