How to Sleep in an Airport During a Long Layover

To sleep in an airport, check its reputation for sleeping first, sleep airside after security in a quiet area, and bring an eye mask, earplugs, a good pillow and a layer. Set several alarms well before boarding. If you must function tomorrow, a lounge or transit hotel is worth it.

  1. Check the airport's reputation for sleeping first.
  2. Sleep airside, after security, in a quiet corner.
  3. Bring an eye mask, earplugs, a good pillow and a layer.
  4. Set several alarms, well before boarding.
  5. If you must function tomorrow, pay for a lounge or transit hotel.

Updated October 9, 2026 · By the DeftBrain team

Sleeping in an airport works best airside, after security, in a quiet area away from food courts and announcements, with an eye mask, earplugs, a proper travel pillow and an extra layer. Some airports, such as Singapore Changi, offer rest zones and transit hotels; others discourage sleeping. Setting several alarms and confirming the gate before sleeping avoids the worst outcome, a missed flight.

You have a 13-hour overnight layover. A hotel feels like a lot of money for a few hours' sleep; the bench feels like a bad night. The difference between some sleep and none is mostly which airport, where you sleep in it, and what you brought.

How to do it
1

Which airports are good for sleeping?

Some airports are widely regarded as good for sleeping (Singapore Changi, Munich, Helsinki, Seoul Incheon — designated sleep areas, dimmed lighting, decent temperature). Some are hostile (any airport with armrests on every bench, frequent floor-cleaning announcements, bright lighting all night). Sites like sleepinginairports.net rank them honestly. If you are arriving at an airport with a poor sleep reputation, plan for a hotel or a transit hotel before you commit to the bench.

2

Where is the best place to sleep in an airport?

Do not sleep in the public arrivals area before security. It is louder, less safe, and often patrolled by police asking people to move. Once you have a boarding pass for an outbound flight, go through security and find the quietest part of the secure area — usually the gates farthest from the food court, near a closed wing or a chapel/prayer room. Some airports have designated sleep zones or rest pods; use them. The quietest 20 minutes you spend exploring the terminal pays off in the best location.

3

What do you need to sleep in an airport?

Eye mask. Earplugs. A good travel pillow. A light blanket or large scarf. A bag you can keep close or use as a pillow. Without these, sleep is unlikely; with them, a few hours is realistic. A basic kit is cheap and lasts for years, so it is worth packing for any long layover. Airport shops usually sell versions at airport prices if you forget.

4

How do you avoid missing your flight if you sleep at the airport?

Sleeping through a flight is the worst-case outcome. Set at least three alarms — phone, watch, second device. Set the first one for 90 minutes before boarding — that is enough time to wake up, hit the bathroom, and get to the gate without panic. Confirm boarding time and gate before you sleep, and re-confirm in the morning (gates change overnight). Tell the gate agent if there is one nearby that you might be sleeping; some will note it and check on you.

5

Is a lounge or transit hotel worth it on a long layover?

If your trip depends on you being functional the next day, paying for sleep is often the right call. Most major airports have day-rate transit hotels (you book by the hour, not the night) where a few hours usually costs much less than a full night. Lounges with sleep pods or recliners (Priority Pass, card-linked, or paid) are another option. The mental cost of arriving at your destination in a fog from a sleepless terminal night is sometimes higher than the cost of just buying real sleep.
