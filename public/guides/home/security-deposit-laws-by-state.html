Security Deposit Laws by State (Limits, Return Deadlines and Interest, 2026)

Each state sets its own security deposit rules. About 30 jurisdictions cap the deposit, usually at one or two months' rent. Return deadlines run from 14 to 60 days after move-out, and 13 jurisdictions require interest in some cases. The table covers all 50 states and DC.

  1. Deposit cap: about 30 jurisdictions cap it, usually at 1 or 2 months' rent.
  2. Return deadline: 14 to 60 days after move-out; 30 days is most common.
  3. Interest: 13 jurisdictions require it, usually with conditions.
  4. Missed deadline: many states bar any deductions and add a penalty.
  5. Give your forwarding address in writing; some deadlines start from it.

The table above gives each state's limit on the deposit, the deadline for returning it, and whether the landlord owes interest, with the statute it comes from. Figures were checked against state statutes and official state sources in November 2026. Most deadlines start when the tenancy ends and you've moved out, and several only start once the landlord has your forwarding address in writing.

Cities and counties can add their own rules, and leases signed before a law changed may follow the old rule. Laws change, so check the statute for your state before you rely on a number. This is general information, not legal advice.

How to do it
1

How much can a landlord charge for a security deposit?

About 30 jurisdictions cap the deposit. The most common caps are one month's rent (Massachusetts, New York, Maryland, Alabama, Hawaii and others) and two months' rent (Colorado, Georgia, Iowa, Missouri, Virginia and others). Nevada allows up to three months. The rest set no cap at all, which leaves the amount to the lease.

Several caps are recent. Colorado set a two-month cap in August 2023. Georgia capped deposits at two months for leases entered or renewed from July 1, 2024. Maryland cut its cap from two months to one for leases signed from October 1, 2024. In states such as Maryland, New York and Georgia, a refundable pet deposit counts toward the cap rather than on top of it.

2

How long does a landlord have to return a security deposit?

Deadlines range from 14 days (New York, Hawaii, Nebraska, Vermont, and Alaska in some cases) to 60 days (Alabama, Arkansas and West Virginia). Thirty days is the most common. By the deadline, the landlord must usually either return the full deposit or send an itemized list of deductions with any balance.

Watch when the clock starts. In Texas, Iowa, South Dakota and Wyoming, among others, the deadline runs from when the landlord has your forwarding address, so give it in writing on the day you move out. Some deadlines changed recently: Washington went from 21 to 30 days in July 2023, Connecticut from 30 to 21 days in October 2023, and South Dakota from 14 to 21 days in July 2026. Kentucky and Tennessee set no fixed number of days.

3

Which states require interest on security deposits?

Interest is the exception. Connecticut, the District of Columbia, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Minnesota, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, North Dakota, Ohio and Pennsylvania require it in at least some cases. Most of them attach conditions, such as a minimum building size (25 or more units in Illinois, 6 or more in New York), a minimum tenancy (two years in Pennsylvania, one year in New Hampshire) or a minimum deposit.

Massachusetts is one of the strictest. The deposit must sit in a separate interest-bearing account at a Massachusetts bank, and interest runs from the first day of the tenancy at 5% a year, or the bank's rate if lower, paid each year or when the tenancy ends. In Florida, interest is owed only if the landlord chooses an interest-bearing account.

4

What happens if a landlord misses the deadline?

In many states, a landlord who misses the deadline loses the right to keep any part of the deposit, even for real damage. New York, Washington and Iowa all work this way. Many states add a penalty for bad-faith or wrongful withholding. Common amounts are two times the amount withheld (Arizona, Missouri and New Jersey, among others) or three times (Georgia and Maryland). Massachusetts awards three times the deposit plus 5% interest, court costs and attorney's fees for its main violations. Texas presumes bad faith after the deadline passes and allows $100 plus three times the amount wrongfully withheld.

These penalties usually require a demand letter or a small-claims case. Your strongest evidence is a dated record of the unit's condition at move-in, which is the subject of how to write a move-in condition report.

5

How do you use your state's rules to get your deposit back?

Start at move-in: photograph every room, note every existing flaw on the condition form, and email both to the landlord. At move-out, give a forwarding address in writing, note the date, and count forward by your state's deadline. If the deadline passes with no refund or itemized list, send a short written demand that cites the statute in the table and the remedy it provides. Many landlords pay at that point.
