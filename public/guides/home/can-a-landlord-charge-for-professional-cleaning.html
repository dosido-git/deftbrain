Can a Landlord Charge for Professional Cleaning When You Move Out?

Only if the unit needs it, and it depends on your state. California bars charging for professional cleaning unless it's reasonably necessary to return the unit to its move-in condition. Massachusetts and New York don't list cleaning among allowed deductions at all.

  1. California: only if reasonably necessary to restore move-in condition.
  2. The standard is move-in cleanliness, not spotless.
  3. Massachusetts and New York don't list cleaning as a deduction.
  4. California landlords must photograph the unit.
  5. Cleaning to move-in level and photographing it is the best defense.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the DeftBrain team

Many leases say the unit must be professionally cleaned at move-out, and many deposit statements include a cleaning fee. Whether that's allowed depends on your state. California's law is the most specific: a landlord can't require you to pay for professional cleaning, or deduct for it, unless it's reasonably necessary to return the unit to its condition at the start of the tenancy, not counting ordinary wear and tear.

Other states differ. Some don't list cleaning as an allowed deduction at all. Rules vary by state and sometimes by city, and leases signed before a change may follow older rules. This is general information, not legal advice.

How to do it
1

Can a landlord require professional cleaning at move-out?

In California, not unless it's reasonably necessary to return the unit to its move-in condition, setting aside ordinary wear and tear. A lease clause requiring a professional clean doesn't override that. If you leave the unit as clean as you found it, a professional-cleaning charge is hard to justify there.

2

Can a landlord deduct for cleaning at all?

It depends on the state. California lets a deposit cover cleaning needed to return the unit to the same level of cleanliness it had at the start of the tenancy. Massachusetts limits deductions to unpaid rent or water charges, certain tax increases and damage beyond reasonable wear and tear, and says no other deductions may be made. New York's list covers unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear and tear, certain utility charges, and moving and storing a tenant's belongings, but not cleaning.

3

What does "move-in level of cleanliness" mean?

It means the unit as clean as it was when you got it, not cleaner. If the oven was greasy on day one, you aren't required to hand it back spotless. That's why a dated move-in record matters: it sets the baseline. California's rule applies to tenancies that began after January 1, 2003.

4

What proof does a landlord need for a cleaning charge?

In California, the itemized statement must include the bill or receipt for outside work, or a description with the time and hourly rate if the landlord did it. California landlords must also photograph the unit at the start of tenancies beginning on or after July 1, 2025, and after move-out before and after any repairs or cleaning they deduct for. Washington requires invoices or receipts too.

5

How do you avoid a cleaning charge?

Clean to at least the move-in standard and photograph every room, appliance and bathroom on move-out day. In California and New York, you can ask for an inspection before you move out so you can fix anything flagged. The security deposit laws by state table lists your state's statute and deadline.

Sources

  • Massachusetts General Laws c. 186, § 15B (Massachusetts Legislature)
  • New York General Obligations Law § 7-108 (NY Senate)
  • RCW 59.18.280 (Washington State Legislature)
