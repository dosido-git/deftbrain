
**Never lead with the count ALONE.** The number is a liability only when it
arrives unaccompanied — "125 AI tools" is indistinguishable from slop. It is
safe, and on Hacker News actively useful, when the same breath disarms it:
*"125 tools, and several will tell you not to use them"* is not a farm claim,
it is an argument. A farm cannot say the second half.

Practical split:
- **Directories and curators** — lead with the problem class. The count belongs
  late or not at all; a curator scanning submissions has no patience for the
  second clause. (An earlier version of this file stated the rule this way and
  then broke it in the HN titles below. This is the correction.)
- **Hacker News** — the count is a hook, because "why would anyone build 125
  instead of one" is a design decision and HN rewards those. Still never on its
  own.

Then earn it with three things a farm never does:

1. **13 languages with jurisdictional and currency reasoning.** Not translation:
   prices, customs and legal parameters change with the reader.
2. **Tools that argue against their own use.** Ticket Tackler says *just pay*.
   BuyWise says *buy the $30 generic instead*. A farm never talks you out of a
   click — and a competitor optimising for engagement structurally cannot copy
   this, which makes it the closest thing to a moat here.
3. **Grounded against live sources** for legal and regulatory facts, with an
   explicit staleness policy — not model memory.

**Anticipated questions — prep (answer honestly, concede fast):**

- *"Why not just use ChatGPT?"* → You can. The tools are the prompt engineering,
  output schema and jurisdiction grounding already done — and the honest-verdict
  design, which a general chatbot won't volunteer. If someone is comfortable
  writing their own prompts, they genuinely don't need this. Say so.
- *"Isn't this just prompt wrappers?"* → Yes, in the sense that a spreadsheet
  is just formulas. The value is in the schema, the calibration, the localisation
  and the refusals. Don't get defensive — it's a fair description.
- *"125 tools sounds like slop."* → The fair version of the question. Point at
  the three anti-farm proofs. Invite them to find one that flatters them into
  using it.
- *"Isn't this legal advice?"* (lease/ticket tools) → Writing and evidence tools;
  they never predict outcomes and the weak-case path tells you to pay. Same
  category as a letter template, with better drafting.
- *"LLMs hallucinate — fake deadlines and portals?"* → Filing facts go through a
  web-verification pre-pass; unverified means the tool says "check the ticket /
  find the official channel", never a specific claim.
- *"Business model?"* → None yet. Validation stage; costs are API calls.
- *"Privacy?"* → No accounts, no cookies, first-party beacon only (path + event,
  no identifiers); inputs go to the model API and are not stored server-side.
- *"DoNotPay comparison?"* → **Corrected 2026-07-31 — an earlier draft here said
  "they promised outcomes and got FTC'd", which is inaccurate, and misstating a
  regulatory action about a named company in public is a bad look.** What
  happened: DoNotPay marketed itself as "the world's first robot lawyer", and
  the FTC's order (finalised Feb 2025, part of Operation AI Comply) turned on
  claims it could **substitute for a lawyer** with no evidence — they never
  tested output against a human lawyer's and never retained an attorney to check
  the legal features. $193K plus notice to 2021–2023 subscribers. Say: *"They
  sold themselves as a robot lawyer and the FTC's order turned on claiming it
  replaced one without evidence. Nothing here claims to be a lawyer — it writes
  YOUR appeal, tells you when to just pay, and guarantees nothing."* Keep every
  capability claim to something you could actually evidence.

# Product Hunt

**Name:** DeftBrain
**Tagline (≤60):** `125 tiny AI tools. Some tell you not to use them.`
**Description:** Small, single-purpose AI tools for problems too specific to
search for — is this lease unfair, is this price fair, what did my doctor
actually say, how do I say the hard thing. Each one is a finished answer, not a
chat box. Several will talk you out of using them. Free, no signup, 13 languages
with local currency and legal norms.

**Maker's first comment:** the Show HN story, lightly warmed up — PH tolerates
more enthusiasm. Keep the "argues against itself" line front and centre; it is
the differentiator on both venues. Close by naming two or three concrete tools
so the breadth lands as *specific things*, not a count.

**Gallery — this is where whole-site submission is hardest, so plan it:**
1. **The JUST PAY verdict** (Ticket Tackler). A tool refusing to be used stops
   the scroll in a feed of AI hype. Lead with it.
2. **BuyWise saying buy the cheaper generic** — same proof, different domain, so
   it reads as a principle rather than a gimmick.
3. **The catalogue**, one clean grid shot. This is the only slide that says
   "there are a lot of these", and it comes third on purpose.
4. **One localised result** side by side with its English twin — the claim that
   is hardest to doubt once seen.

**Mechanics**
- Publish a **Coming Soon page 1–2 weeks ahead.** Followers are auto-notified at
  launch and ranking is momentum-weighted on the first 2–3 hours. This is the
  only way to manufacture first-hour momentum without an audience, and it is the
  step solo makers skip.
- **Go live 12:01am PT** for the full 24-hour window.
- **Tue–Thu** for traffic, weekend for an easier top-5. Validation is the goal,
  so take the weekday traffic.
- **Never ask for upvotes** — penalised and detectable. "I'd love your feedback"
  gets better comments anyway.
- **Comments are weighted heavily** and are the part you control. Reply to every
  one, fast, all day.
- PH changes its mechanics regularly — **verify current rules before committing.**

**Expectations, honestly:** PH rewards distribution you already have. With ~2
backlinks and no list, top-5 is unlikely and only ~10% of submissions get
homepage-featured. Realistic outcome: a few hundred visitors, a `rel="ugc"` link
from a high-authority domain, and the first real feedback from strangers. Worth
doing — a modest result is not a verdict on the product.

# Community answers (Reddit / Quora / forums)

Legitimate, with one rule: **say you built it.** One clause. Quora requires
conflict-of-interest disclosure outright; Reddit and HN don't formally, but
omitting it turns a contribution into spam.

- Answer the question. In a "favourite AI tools" thread, name two or three you
  genuinely use and include yours. Recommending only your own reads as an ad
  regardless of disclosure.
- **Never use a second account.** Astroturfing is the one thing that is actually
  not kosher and the thing communities punish hardest.
- Check the subreddit's own rules — many ban self-promo or confine it to a
  weekly thread.
- **Build a little history first.** A new account whose first posts all link
  deftbrain.com gets filtered, and Reddit shadowbans are invisible: you keep
  posting, nobody sees it, and you wrongly conclude nobody cared.

These links are nofollow, so this will not move Google. The value is real humans
plus prose mentions in the corpora AI search retrieves from — a different lever,
and one currently at zero.

# Sequencing

1. **Directories first.** A PH or HN visitor who searches your name and finds
   you already listed elsewhere reads as established.
2. **Show HN**, weekday morning ET. Let it breathe.
3. **Product Hunt** a week later — gallery prepped, Coming Soon page live 1–2
   weeks before *that*, so start it during step 1.

**Before any of it — two blockers:**

- **API billing auto-reload must be ON.** On 2026-07-30 the credit balance hit
  zero and every tool returned 500 while `/api/health` still reported `ok`.
  During a launch window that burns the one shot. Consider making the health
  check fail loudly when the models are unreachable.
- **Flag your own browsers with `?operator=1`** so the traffic spike is clean.

**Measure the right thing:** watch `tool_run` and interactive sessions, not
referrers. A returning visitor is worth more than a hundred bouncing ones —
which is why the *frequently needed* tools (MiseEnPlace, VirtualBodyDouble,
ContextCollapse, TheDebrief, DecoderRing) are the retention instruments, while
Ticket Tackler is the demo. Guide readership began reporting 2026-08-02, so an
empty earlier window means "not measured", not "not read".
